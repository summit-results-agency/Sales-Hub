Section 01

Start Here

Your job is not to read a script or fill a calendar. Your job is to have good business conversations and turn the right opportunities into clients.

Own the sale

Keep moving while it makes sense

If the owner is engaged and has time, do not stop just because you reached the point where an SDR would normally book a meeting. Continue into the product, value, price, objections, and close.

The best next step is the furthest reasonable next step.

Keep it natural

Frameworks, not memorized lines

Use the language in this hub when it helps. Change wording, skip questions you already have answers to, and follow what the prospect is actually saying.

A natural conversation with clear direction beats a perfect script.

The priority order

1Close now

If the prospect has the time, interest, and fit, finish the sale.

2Book yourself

If they need more time, a proper demo, or another decision maker, schedule your own follow-up.

3Bring in the team

Use a team handoff when the deal needs technical scoping, special approval, or support beyond your authority.

Do not force a bad fit. Some businesses genuinely do not have enough call volume, margin, or need for the product. Find out. If the economics do not make sense, move on professionally.

Section 07

Objection Handling

Do not memorize clever rebuttals. Find out what the objection actually means, respond to that, and move the conversation back toward a decision.

1Diagnose

"What makes you say that?"

2Understand

Listen for the real concern.

3Reframe

Use their situation and numbers.

4Confirm

"Does that address it?"

5Close

Move back to the decision.

"$397 is too expensive."

"Got it. What makes you say that?"

Do not defend the price until you know what they mean. Often the real answer is that they do not believe they miss enough calls, they do not see enough customer value, or they are comparing the number to a cheaper alternative.

"That's fair. Let's see if it even makes sense. You said you're missing about [X] calls and an average customer is worth about $[Y]. If solving even part of that creates more than $397 in value, then it makes sense. If it doesn't, I wouldn't tell you to buy it either."
"It's also about $13 a day for unlimited 24/7 call handling. The bigger question is whether the calls it helps capture are worth more than that."

"We don't miss enough calls."

"That may be true. About how many would you say actually go unanswered in a day or week?"

Use the real number. If the economics are weak, do not manufacture urgency. If even a few missed calls are valuable, work through that together.

"Another company is cheaper."

"Absolutely, there are cheaper options. What are they including at that price?"

Compare scope, not logos. Ask about call limits, per-minute charges, simultaneous calls, customization, scheduling, integrations, and what happens when volume increases.

"If their setup does everything you need at that price, that may be a good option. I just want to make sure we're comparing the same thing."

"I don't want AI answering my calls."

"I understand. It doesn't have to replace the way you answer calls now. Your team can still answer like normal. The AI can mainly step in when nobody is available, so the alternative is usually AI versus voicemail, not AI versus your staff."

"I need to think about it."

"Of course. What specifically do you feel like you need to think through?"

Do not accept a vague stall without understanding it. If the concern is price, product, timing, partner approval, or trust, deal with the real issue. If they genuinely need time, schedule the next conversation with yourself.

"I need to talk to my partner."

"Makes sense. Is there anything you think they'll want to know before making the decision?"

Answer what you can, then schedule a specific follow-up with both decision makers whenever possible.

When price is the only blocker

Do the value work first. If they genuinely want the product and the only thing stopping the sale is price, find the number that changes the decision.

"If I can get $___ approved, will you move forward today?" Get the commitment before requesting approval. Do not promise the discount yourself unless you have explicit authority.

Know when to stop. Objection handling is for real opportunities with a solvable concern. It is not a contest to argue an uninterested person into submission.

Section 09

Follow-Up & Appointments

A follow-up is not a handoff. If you schedule the next conversation, it stays your opportunity unless there is a specific reason to involve someone else.

Your calendar

Schedule prospects with yourself

Use your assigned Summit Results sales calendar. Book the time while you still have the prospect on the phone whenever possible.

"No problem. Let's put 15 minutes on my calendar when you have more time and we'll pick it back up there."
Your email

Own the communication

Use your Summit Results company email for prospect follow-up, confirmations, requested information, and deal communication.

Keep messages short and focused on the agreed next step.

Before ending a scheduled follow-up

Specific date and time agreed
Correct decision maker attending
Phone number and email confirmed
Reason for the follow-up is clear
CRM notes capture the problem, value, objections, and next step

Team appointments

Booking another closer or manager should be uncommon. Use it when the prospect needs something you cannot reasonably resolve yourself, not simply because the conversation reached the closing stage.
