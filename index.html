Section 02

Company Overview

Summit Results Agency helps businesses use AI to capture more customers and revenue. Our two services are websites and an AI receptionist.

The problem we solve

Businesses spend money and effort getting customers to call them. Ads, trucks, signs, referrals, Google, all of it points to the phone ringing.

When nobody answers, those calls go to voicemail.

Most customers will not leave a voicemail. They hang up and call the next business on the list.

Summit Results Agency helps businesses capture those opportunities instead of losing them.

Remember this
A missed call is not a missed call. It is a customer who just called your competitor.

How to position us

Lead with this

Business owners care about outcomes:

  • Capturing more customers
  • Avoiding missed opportunities
  • Capturing revenue that would have been lost
  • Booking more appointments
  • Qualifying leads
  • Responding to customers when nobody is available
  • Making sure valuable calls get handled

Do not lead with this

Do not position the product as a way to replace employees or cut receptionist workload.

That framing puts the owner on the defensive, makes it about their staff, and moves the conversation away from money.

Keep it on business outcomes. Missed calls, lost customers, lost revenue, more booked jobs.

Who we work with

Local businesses that depend on calls and a credible online presence. Home services, auto shops, law firms, contractors, and similar operations where a ringing phone is a potential job.

How we operate

We build and maintain websites as well as AI receptionists. The website helps customers find and trust a business; the receptionist answers and books the calls that follow.

Summit Results Agency runs a defined process. Sales, demo build, implementation, and client onboarding are all separate, structured steps handled by the team. When you book a qualified appointment, there is a real process waiting behind it.

Cold Call Scripts

Three openers. After the opener, ask about missed calls, explain the receptionist and listen, then work through the ticket value and price before offering a custom demo.

These are guides, not lines to recite. Change the wording, skip questions, and follow the conversation. Sounding natural beats sounding accurate.

Script 1: Free AI Audit

1
Opening
If needed:
"Hey, is this [Business Name]?"
Then:
"Hey, this is [Name] with Summit Results Agency. Would you happen to be the owner?"
If yes:
"Perfect. I'll be quick. Again, this is [Name] with Summit Results Agency. Not sure if you've heard our name before, but we're working with [industry] businesses in [area], and right now we're offering free AI audits to see how AI could actually help your business."
Stop talking and let them respond. If they ask what the audit means: "We look at where calls may be slipping through and whether an AI receptionist could help."
2
Ask about their calls
Ask these naturally and listen after each answer. Get the missed-call number in the period they know best.
"About how many calls go unanswered or to voicemail on a normal day or week?"
"Who normally handles the phone for you?"
"What happens to those callers when nobody can pick up?"
If they say none are missed, explore whether after-hours calls or busy periods are different. Do not invent a problem.
3
Explain it, then listen
"That's why I reached out. We build AI receptionists that answer when your team can't. It can talk to callers, answer common questions, collect their details, and help book appointments, so those calls don't just go to voicemail."
Stop and let them respond. Address what they actually say before moving into the numbers. If they are not interested, do not force the rest of the script.
4
Ask the ticket, then calculate together
"Roughly what does an average new customer spend with you?"
Wait for their answer. Use the missed-call number they already gave you. Confirm it once, then enter both numbers in the calculator.
"You mentioned about [number] missed calls [day/week/month], and a new customer is worth about $[amount]. Did I get that right?"
"If even 20 to 30 percent of those calls became customers, that could be roughly $[low] to $[high] in gross sales a month. Does that seem realistic for your business?"
Let them agree or correct the assumptions. This is a possible opportunity, not guaranteed revenue. If they cannot give credible numbers, skip the math.
"The AI receptionist is $397 a month. At your average ticket, [break-even count] extra customer(s) would cover that monthly price in gross sales. Would capturing more of those calls be useful?"
If they ask about price earlier, answer right away. Do not wait until this step.
5
Offer the custom demo and check intent
"If this looks useful, our team can look at your website and business, set up a custom demo, and show you exactly how it would handle your calls."
"If it does what you need and the pricing makes sense, would you be open to moving forward?"
Pause. An honest "yes, if it works" is enough. If they cannot consider the price or have no intention to implement, do not book a custom build just for curiosity. Clarify the concern or offer the live demo if appropriate.
6
Book and set expectations
"Would you have about 15 minutes later today or tomorrow morning to see it?"
If neither works, ask: "When would you have about 15 minutes?"

Qualification & ROI

A custom demo is for a decision maker with a real problem, a plausible return, and a willingness to consider $397/month if the product fits.

Keep it conversational. Ask one question, listen, and use their own numbers. A positive estimate alone does not qualify a prospect.

Qualify before you book

1 · Decision maker

Can they make or influence this decision?

"Would you be the person deciding how calls are handled, or would someone else need to join us?" If someone else decides, include them in the demo.

2 · Problem

Are valuable calls actually being missed?

"About how many calls go unanswered or to voicemail in a day or week? What happens to those callers?" Clarify whether they are potential new customers, and whether their team already calls them back successfully. Not every missed call is a lost sale.

3 · Value

What is a new customer worth?

"Roughly what does a typical new customer spend?" Use the average first sale unless they give a reliable lifetime value. If uncertain, say the estimate is rough.

4 · Timing and fit

Would they use this if it works?

"If we show you it can handle those calls the way you want, is this something you would look to put in place soon?" Learn about any deadline, approval, phone setup, or requirement for the demo.

Section 09

Objection Handling

Do not fight every objection.

Your time matters. If someone is clearly uninterested, hostile, or has no desire to continue the conversation, be polite, end the call, and move on. It is not worth spending several minutes trying to convince somebody who is completely against the idea. The next dial is worth more than that argument.

When to actually use objection handling

Use it when there appears to be a legitimate opportunity, but the prospect has a concern, hesitation, misconception, or question stopping them from taking the next step.

Do not become argumentative. The goal is to clear up the concern and move back toward the demo when it makes sense.

Objection: "I don't want AI answering my calls."

"I completely understand. The way we normally set this up is so the calls can still go to you guys first. If you're available to answer, that's great, you answer it like you normally would."
"If you're not available, instead of that customer going to voicemail, the call can roll over to the AI. The AI can answer the customer, collect their information, qualify them, and help get them booked."
"So the calls you would normally answer still get answered by you. We're mainly helping with the calls that would have gone to voicemail anyway."
"And I'm sure you can agree, having the AI help that customer is still much better than potentially losing the customer altogether."

Then transition naturally back toward the demo.

Do not argue about whether AI is better than a human. That is not the point. The point is that AI acts as a backup when a human is unavailable.

Objection: "We're too small."

"I completely understand. We actually work with smaller businesses for exactly that reason."
"When you're a smaller business, every customer matters more. A larger company might be able to afford to lose a few opportunities. When you're smaller, you want to make sure you're capturing as much business as possible and not leaving money on the table."

Then transition back into their current missed call situation or the demo.

Other objections and detailed questions

If the prospect asks detailed questions about any of these:

  • Custom pricing or discounts
  • Contracts
  • Technical integrations
  • CRM integrations
  • Setup details
  • Complex workflows
  • Implementation
  • Anything you are unsure about

Do not invent an answer.

Share the published price clearly. If you do not know a custom or technical answer, say the team can cover it on the demo. Never make up an answer or promise an unapproved term.

"The AI receptionist is $397 a month. That particular setup question is something our team can check on the demo. If it works for your business, would you be open to moving forward at that price?"

Again, do not try to overcome every objection. Only keep going when there is genuine interest or a reasonable opportunity.

Section 10

What Makes a Good Appointment

A booked slot is not the same thing as a qualified opportunity. Here is the standard.

A good appointment generally means

The prospect is the owner, decision maker, manager, or someone meaningfully involved in the decision.
They understand at a basic level what Summit Results Agency does.
You have discussed missed calls, what happens to them, and an approximate customer value when available.
There is a possible problem Summit Results Agency could solve.
They know the AI receptionist is $397/month and could consider that price if the demo fits.
They have a reason to act and would consider moving forward if the demo solves their problem.
The appointment time is legitimate.
They understand the team will spend time preparing something specifically for their business.
They have agreed to notify us if they need to reschedule.

You do not need a promise to buy before the demo. You do need a real price conversation and an honest indication that they might move forward if the demo works. If they are ready to try it now, use the live demo path.

Why the bar matters

Good appointment

The team invests real time researching the business and building a custom receptionist. The prospect shows up, sees something built for them, and there is a genuine chance of a close. That is how you get paid.

Bad appointment

Someone agreed to a time just to get you off the phone. The team burns hours building for a no show. Nothing closes and your number looks good on paper while you earn nothing.
